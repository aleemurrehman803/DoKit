Touch typing in 30 days: a realistic plan

10-minute read · October 2026

Let's be real: you won't hit 80 WPM in a month if you're starting from hunt-and-peck. But 30 days of focused practice will get you typing without looking at the keyboard at 25–40 WPM — and that's life-changing for anyone who writes daily.

The plan below assumes 20–30 minutes of practice per day. Consistency beats marathons: 25 minutes daily outperforms a 3-hour weekend binge.

The 4-week plan

  • Week 1 — Home row (days 1–7). Learn A S D F J K L ; and nothing else. Your fingers live here; every other key is a reach from home base. Practice accuracy first, speed later. Target: type the home row blindfolded (metaphorically).
  • Week 2 — Top row (days 8–14). Add Q W E R T Y U I O P. This is the hardest week — your fingers will feel clumsy and you'll want to peek. Don't. Slow, correct practice builds the muscle memory; fast, sloppy practice builds bad habits.
  • Week 3 — Bottom row + common words (days 15–21). Add Z X C V B N M, then drill the 100 most common English words (the, be, to, of, and…). These words make up half of everything you type — mastering them is a massive speed boost.
  • Week 4 — Real text (days 22–30). Stop drilling letters; start typing real sentences and paragraphs. Use typing tests to measure WPM and accuracy. Aim for 95%+ accuracy — speed follows accuracy automatically.

Five rules that make it work

  • Never look at the keyboard. Cover your hands with a towel if you must. Peeking resets your progress.
  • Accuracy over speed, always. If accuracy drops below 90%, slow down. Fast mistakes teach your fingers the wrong thing.
  • Keep fingers on home row. After every keystroke, return to base. This is the whole game.
  • Short sessions, every day. 25 focused minutes beats 2 distracted hours.
  • Track your problem keys. Smart practice drills target the keys you miss most — use them.

Seven mistakes that slow everyone down

  1. Practicing too fast. Speed is a byproduct of accuracy, not a goal. If your accuracy is under 92%, you're going too fast.
  2. Looking "just this once". Every peek teaches your brain that the keyboard is the source of truth instead of your fingers.
  3. Ignoring the pinkies. The pinkies handle A, ;, Q, P, and Shift — roughly 15% of keystrokes. Weak pinkies cap your speed permanently.
  4. Skipping the boring drills. Muscle memory is built by repetition, and there's no shortcut around it.
  5. Practicing while tired. Fatigue destroys form. A focused 20 minutes beats a sloppy 60.
  6. Wrong keyboard height. Wrists should be straight, not bent up or down.
  7. Quitting in week 2. Week 2 is the valley: you're slower than your old hunt-and-peck and it feels like regression. It's not — it's your brain rewiring.

Ergonomics: set up once, benefit for years

  • Elbows at ~90°: forearms roughly parallel to the floor. Adjust chair height first, then keyboard.
  • Wrists straight and floating: don't rest your wrists on the desk while typing.
  • Screen at eye level: the top of the screen at or slightly below eye height, about an arm's length away.
  • Laptop users: prop the laptop on a stand and use an external keyboard.
  • Take micro-breaks: every 25 minutes, shake out your hands for 30 seconds.

How to measure progress (beyond WPM)

Words per minute is the headline number, but it's noisy. Track accuracy trend (should climb toward 95%+), problem keys (typically pinky keys Q, P, Z and reaches like B, Y), consistency across paragraphs, and real-world speed on unfamiliar text — usually 10–15 lower than your best drill score. Benchmarks: 25 WPM with 95% accuracy after 30 days is solid. 40 WPM puts you ahead of the average office worker. 60+ WPM is genuinely fast.

After day 30: the maintenance phase

Day 30 isn't the finish line — it's the point where practice becomes optional because daily life takes over. Type everything for real, take one speed test per week, spend a week on the numbers row, and try typing games for speed under pressure. Most people reach 50–60 WPM within 3–6 months of normal daily typing after the initial 30 days.
