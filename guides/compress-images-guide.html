How to compress images

Make photos dramatically smaller for websites, email and messaging — without visible quality loss.

Open Image Compressor

Steps

  1. 1. Add your images

    Drop JPG, PNG or WebP files into the tool. Batch mode handles many files at once.

  2. 2. Set a target size or quality

    Either pick a quality level (e.g. 80%) or set a target file size in KB and let DoKit find the best quality that fits.

  3. 3. Compare before & after

    Zoom into details to confirm the compressed version still looks clean — text edges and faces matter most.

  4. 4. Convert format if it helps

    WebP is usually 25–35% smaller than JPEG at the same quality. Consider converting while you compress.

  5. 5. Download the results

    Download files individually or all at once. Originals on your device are never modified.

Pro tips

  • 80% JPEG quality is the sweet spot for photos — much smaller, visually identical.
  • Compress before uploading to websites and CMSs — pages load faster and rank better.
  • Keep a master copy. Compression is lossy; archive the original before shrinking.

Frequently asked questions

Will people notice the quality difference?

At sensible settings (JPEG ~80%, WebP ~75%), no. Pixel-peeping at 200% zoom might reveal tiny differences, but normal viewing won't.

Can I hit an exact KB target?

Yes — set a target size in KB and the tool adjusts quality automatically to fit. PNG files can't always reach very small targets; convert to JPEG/WebP instead.

Is compression safe for print?

For print, keep quality at 90%+ and don't downscale. For screen use, 70–80% is plenty.
