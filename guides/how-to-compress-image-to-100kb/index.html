How to Compress an Image to 100KB

Forms and portals reject oversized photos. Here's how to hit an exact KB target without ruining quality.

Why 100KB?

Government forms, exam portals and job applications commonly cap photos at 50KB, 100KB or 200KB. A phone photo is 3–8 MB — 20–80× too large — so this isn't a small trim, it's a real conversion job.

Step 1: Resize first, compress second

Compression works far better on a sensibly-sized image. If a portal wants a 600×600 photo, resize to 600×600 first — then compress. Compressing a 4000px photo to 100KB forces brutal quality loss that resizing avoids.

Step 2: Pick the right format

For photographs, JPEG or WebP. WebP is typically 25–35% smaller than JPEG at the same quality. PNG is lossless, so it can't be squeezed the same way — for a strict KB target, WebP is usually the answer.

Step 3: Search for the quality that hits the target

There is no single "correct" quality percentage — it depends on the photo. The reliable method is a search: try a quality level, check the size, adjust up or down, and repeat until you land at or under the target. DoKit's Image Compressor does this search for you: pick 50KB, 100KB, 200KB or your own target, and it finds the quality that fits.

If it still won't fit

  • Reduce the dimensions a little more — a 10% smaller image is often invisible, but saves a lot of bytes.
  • Switch to WebP output.
  • Crop empty background if the photo allows it.

Check before you submit

Always verify two things: the file is under the limit, and the photo still looks acceptable at 100% zoom. A photo that passes the size check but looks like mush will still get rejected by a human reviewer.
