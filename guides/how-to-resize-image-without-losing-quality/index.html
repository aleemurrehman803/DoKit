How to Resize an Image Without Losing Quality

The rules that actually matter — and the mistakes that ruin photos.

Rule 1: Keep the aspect ratio locked

Stretching an image to fit a box is the fastest way to ruin it. Always resize width and height together, with the aspect ratio locked. If you need a different shape, crop — don't squash.

Rule 2: Only scale down, never up

Enlarging a small image invents pixels that were never there, so it turns soft and blurry. Start from the largest original you have, and scale down to the size you need.

Rule 3: Resize from the original, every time

Each resize-and-save round loses a little quality. Keep your original safe, and always produce the resized copy from it — never resize an already-resized file.

Rule 4: Save once, in the final format

Every save in a lossy format (JPEG, WebP) costs quality. Do all your edits first, then save once at the end, in the format you'll actually publish.

Pick the right format

  • JPG for photos — small files, great for photographs.
  • PNG for graphics — sharp text and logos, plus transparency.
  • WebP for the web — typically 25–35% smaller than JPG at the same quality, supported by every modern browser.

What size do you actually need?

Match the display size: a hero image at 1600–1920px wide, article images at 800–1200px, thumbnails at 400–600px. A 4000px phone photo displayed at 800px wastes 5× the data for zero visible benefit.
